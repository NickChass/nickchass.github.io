01 / QUESTION

When does distress become a reason to drink?

My research interests centre on how people interpret situations, how they feel, and the behaviour that follows. This project develops that question in substance-use research: does daily negative affect predict alcohol use, and does the relationship differ for people who report drinking to cope?

The distinction matters. Distress is an emotional state, appraisal is an interpretation of a situation, and an expectation of alcohol relief is a belief about what drinking will do. This reanalysis measures affect and baseline coping motives. The proposed next study measures appraisal and relief expectations directly.

02 / ANALYSIS

Separate the decision to drink from positive alcohol counts

The source data were collected and processed by Dora and colleagues (2026). Their young-adult community cohort contributed 15,872 scheduled participant-days. The actual included sample varies across model specifications and imputations.

The analysis compares daily-mean and last eligible negative-affect reports, covariate adjustment, a common-sample comparison, and baseline coping-motive interactions across five supplied imputations. Outcome-related sensitivities examine observed alcohol reports and imputed counts above the observed instrument limit. Ten additional models explicitly separate retained daily deviations from residual participant means.

All 60 models converged with positive-definite Hessians. Separate coding, pooling and optimizer checks agreed with the reported estimates. Those checks verify numerical implementation; they do not establish causal effects.

03 / FINDING

Adjustment changes the association

The unadjusted daily-mean drinking odds ratio was 0.921 [0.872, 0.974]. After adjustment it was 0.987 [0.936, 1.042]. The common-sample comparison shows that losing rows alone does not explain this difference. Coping interaction intervals include zero, and the additional within-person centring check gives a similar interpretation.

The findings do not establish a reliable general positive distress–drinking association in these focused models. They also do not establish that distress is irrelevant to addiction. Appraisal, immediate relief expectations, diagnosed alcohol-use disorder and treatment response were not measured.

04 / NEXT STUDY

Measure the proposed process prospectively

The proposed study would measure event threat, controllability and perceived coping resources alongside distress, anticipated alcohol relief, craving, availability and prior intention to drink. Later prompts would record use in a defined future interval. A feasibility pilot would guide measurement refinement and sample-size simulation.

This protocol has no recruited participants or participant findings. Temporal prediction would be distinguished from causal mediation, and a clinical sample would require its own recruitment and assessment strategy.
