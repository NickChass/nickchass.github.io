Consent and debrief drafts

Nicholai Chasseau · 3 October 2026 · Proposed protocol 0.1.0-draft

These are drafts for review. They are not approved consent documents, do not authorize recruitment, and must not be presented as institutionally approved. No participant collection is enabled in the current local instrument. Final researcher/review contacts, recruitment population, compensation, private return channel, storage/retention and withdrawal arrangements depend on a future reviewed study and are intentionally not invented here. The protocol and materials define the task.

Current development-demo acknowledgment

The public local tool should distinguish an acknowledgment of a technical demonstration from research consent:

This is an unpiloted development demonstration of a proposed study, not an active participant study. It uses fictional teaching scenarios and an assigned scoring rule so the procedure can be inspected. The current code keeps this run's answers, choices and relative timing in browser memory. No information is sent to a research server, and participant collection is disabled.

You can stop this development run at any time. Stopping tags the run withdrawn and excludes it from analytic outcome estimates; its in-memory technical history remains available for optional inspection. Resetting or refreshing clears that history. Export is optional and downloads a clearly labeled development JSON file to your device. It does not upload or submit the file. A downloaded file remains on your device until you delete it yourself. Please keep any export private; do not put it in the public project repository.

I understand that I am inspecting a development demonstration and that this acknowledgment is not consent to an approved research study.

The acknowledgment event is demo_acknowledged with research_consent: false. Current exports are labeled development_demo or synthetic, never participant findings. The local tool does not collect names, contact details or IP addresses, and its timing fields are relative elapsed milliseconds. The session label describes a technical run, not an authenticated person.

Debrief text — draft for the proposed study

Thank you for considering this task. The study compares two accurate scoring rules. Under all-correct credit, every correct practice answer earns a point, including a correct repetition. Under distinct-item credit, each item earns one point when first answered correctly; another correct answer to that item adds no point. Both groups use the same teaching and begin with one point for the guided warmup.

We are interested in whether the rule changes the first practice action people choose and how they perform on new examples afterward. We ask separately about goals, interest, enjoyment, competence and pressure. Choosing a repeat does not establish that someone was wasting effort or deliberately pursuing points instead of learning. Repetition can be useful, and reported goals can coexist.

The later questions do not earn practice points. They are original, unvalidated assessment items, not a diagnosis, verified mastery score or established measure of learning gain. A difference between scoring rules would apply to this task and comparison; it would not by itself show that all digital metrics help or harm motivation.

All teaching scenarios were fictional. No false performance benchmark or fabricated comparison with other people was used. If you wish to review the numerical explanations after finishing, the study materials provide them.
